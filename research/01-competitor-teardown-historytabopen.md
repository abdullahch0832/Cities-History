## 3. Where do the topics come from? (the "scraping" source)

They do not scrape footage. They **copy proven topics**:

1. **Proven outliers from bigger "Entire History" channels.** This Is History's top outliers are *Japan (3.1M), Jerusalem (2.3M), London (1.5M), Ottoman Empire (1.8M)*. HistoryTabOpen made Israel (Jerusalem's sibling topic) and Japan, both already validated.
2. **News cycle.** Israel, Iron Dome and Trump are all high-search news entities in 2026. Evergreen history framing plus trending keyword gives both search and browse traffic.
3. **Same-title templating.** Every title is `The ENTIRE History of {X} in 10 Minutes`, which matches the long-running Knowledgia / History Matters "in 10 minutes" format (Knowledgia "History of the US in 10 minutes" 4M, History Matters "Ten Minute History" 3.1M).

**Replication for cities:** take city outliers from This Is History, Majestic Studios, Midtown History and Lost British Cities, then add cities that are in the news (see `02-city-niche-data.md`).

## 4. Title pattern

```
The ENTIRE History of {Subject} in 10 Minutes
```
- The CAPS word "ENTIRE" carries the promise.
- The time box ("10 Minutes") lowers the commitment barrier.
- No subtitle and no emoji.
- **Weakness:** comments attack the promise ("If you use the word Entire, it should be thousands of years ago", 103 likes). The Israel video stops at the Crusaders and promises a Part 2. That drives comments (good for the algorithm) but annoys some viewers.

## 5. Script pattern (reverse-engineered from transcripts)

Pace: **~140–150 words/minute**, so a 9-minute video is ~1,300 words. (Majestic Studios' London script is slower, ~124 wpm.)

| # | Beat | Time | Israel example | Japan example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cold open: exact timestamped scene** | 0:00–0:50 | "At 4:00 in the afternoon on May 14th, 1948, a man stood beneath a portrait of Theodor Herzl…" | "On August 6th, 1945, at 8:15 in the morning…" |
| 2 | **Paradox / impossible fact** | 0:50–1:30 | "And somehow, that state is still here… this is the strange thing about Israel. The country is tiny… yet almost every great empire passed through this strip of land." | "And yet within a generation, Japan would become one of the world's largest economies." |
| 3 | **"Here is what should be impossible"** recap of the whole arc in 30s | 1:30–2:30 | Babylon → Rome → Crusaders → Ottomans → British → 1948 | (merged with #4) |
| 4 | **Question stack (5–6 open loops)** | 2:30–3:15 | "So how did…? Why did…? Why did Britain…? How did a UN partition become a war?…" | "So how did a collection of islands… become…?" |
| 5 | **Rewind triad** | ~3:15 | "To understand any of that, we have to go back before Israel, before Palestine, before Rome…" | "…long before the samurai, before Tokyo, before Japan even had a name." |
| 6 | **Geography first (map beat)** | 3:25–4:00 | "This land sits at a geographical bottleneck. To the west… to the east…" | "Japan's story begins with geography. Four major islands…" |
| 7 | **Chronological eras**, each ending in a forward-pull line | 4:00–end | "Then, Babylon became the dominant power." / "Jerusalem was about to become sacred to another world religion." | "Japan was about to enter the world of East Asian empires." / "…something arrived that would change warfare forever." |
| 8 | **Myth-busting lines** for credibility and comments | throughout | "The Western Wall… is not the surviving wall of the Temple building itself" | "But forget the image of every samurai as a wandering swordsman" |
| 9 | **Mid-roll subscribe CTA** | ~6:30 (Japan), 0:17 (Iron Dome) | – | "A fast reminder guys, don't forget to subscribe… Now, let's continue." |
| 10 | **Cliffhanger end → Part 2** | last 20s | "subscribe… so you can catch the second part" | "Find out in part two of the entire history of Japan coming soon." |

Other script traits:
- Short declarative sentences ("It did not last." "Rome crushed it.")
- Lists of three ("conquest, displacement, memory, return")
- **Balanced framing on contested topics** (it names both Jewish and Palestinian history). This keeps the video advertiser-friendly and makes both sides comment.
- Archaeology versus tradition contrast ("The biblical texts tell… Archaeology gives us a more fragmentary picture").

## 6. Description pattern (Israel video)

1. **SEO hook paragraph** with the main keyword twice ("The history of Israel… This Israel history documentary covers…").
2. **Stakes paragraph** with a "wow" stat ("1.5 million years old").
3. **Keyword-dense era list.** One long sentence naming 25+ entities (Jericho, Canaanite city-states, First Temple, Babylonian Exile, Dead Sea Scrolls, Maccabean Revolt…). This is how they rank for long-tail searches.
4. **Dramatic closing paragraph** ("The Babylonians could not erase its identity…").
5. **Credibility line:** "based on archaeological discoveries, peer-reviewed research, and primary historical sources."
6. **5 academic citations** (author, year, title, publisher).
7. **14 hashtags.**
8. **Tags (19):** misspellings included on purpose ("isreal", "history of isreal"), plus "history summarized" and "animated history".

## 8. Strengths to copy and weaknesses to exploit

**Copy:**
- Timestamped cold open, paradox, question stack, rewind triad. This hook formula held viewers on a 1M-view video.
- A 10-minute time box and an illustrated, map-driven visual identity.
- A news-adjacent topic choice.
- The academic-looking description with citations.

**Exploit:**
- "ENTIRE" over-promises, and viewers complain. For cities you can actually cover founding to today in 10–15 min, so deliver the full arc.
- Channel identity is broad (countries, a weapon, a politician). A **cities-only** channel builds a clearer audience and a binge-able series ("Cities of the World" playlist).
- Weak captions (`hasCaption: false`). Upload proper SRT files for search and accessibility.
